Ticket 101: Bulldog tee

customer order
Requester
Tauhid Zaman
Request
"Needs a tee in size S."

CC-TEE-WHITE (Classic Bulldog Tee), size S, qty 1

Expected written before the run

Who the Boss calls first, and why

The boss calls inventory first because it needs to know whether the order can be filled as is.

Expected steps and delegations

  1. The boss will read the ticket and check the date.
  2. It will call inventory for stock on the item and will get the response that stock is 0 in size S and return stock for the other sizes.
  3. The boss will call accounting to check the linked invoice 501 and accounting will check current balance, find an outstanding invoice of $840 that needs to be paid before the reprint order will be handle, and identify any other outstanding invoices and due dates.
  4. Accounting will call facilities to check rent obligations.
  5. Facilities finds the outstanding rent due Sept 2 and returns that information to accounting.
  6. Accounting determines that paying the $840 and the $2400 rent due will leave $160 so prepares payment of the $840 for human approval and returns that to the boss with the information it has gathered including a priority of paying the overdue $840 while noting the upcoming rent due.
  7. The boss asks customer service to draft a response to the customer to let them know about the delay due to out of stock and ask about suitability of a different size that is currently in stock.
  8. Customer service calls inventory to confirm the information from the boss, writes the draft and sends it back to the boss while informing the boss if there is any unconfirmed information.
  9. The boss will update the ticket status with waiting_on_approval and will draft the request to the human (i.e., approval to pay the $840 invoice and showing the draft e-mail for the human to send if desired).

Tools expected (MCP tools plus ask_agent for handoffs)

  • Boss: get_ticket, get_desk, ask_agent, update_ticket
  • Inventory: check_stock, list_vendors, check_vendor_invoices
  • Accounting: check_stock, check_vendor_invoices, get_invoice, get_cash_balance, prepare_payment, ask_agent
  • Facilities: get_lease, get_desk
  • Customer Service: ask_agent

Reflection

How did the agents perform on each ticket, and why?

The agents overall did well but there were some issues. They handled the first two tickets without any problems from the agents, presenting an approval request to a human with the appropriate information. However, in the second ticket I was allowed to approve it before the agents had fully finished, resulting in the ticket showing as resolved at the time the agents finished. For the third ticket there was an issue that didn't mess with the final result - a vendor look up created a false vendor that was then rejected.

How did Actual compare to the Expected plan?

Actual compared overall well to expected but with notable differences.

  • Ticket 101: I thought accounting would ask facilities about rent because I neglected to notice that accounting could do it itself. Also customer service did not ask inventory to confirm what the boss told it about inventory, and the draft to Tauhid did not offer the tee in a size that is in stock (M 5, L 3, XL 2).
  • Ticket 102: There was the issue with final status being resolved because I was able to approve the request before the agents fully finished their run and that approval was acknowledged in media res by the agents. I also did not anticipate accounting using get_lease and erroneously listed check_vendor_invoices.
  • Ticket 103: Accounting never checked the cash so the expected message about the $264 restock expense (12 × $22) being more than the $160 balance didn't happen. The Cash tab shows only $160 was left after the $840 invoice (101) and the $2,400 rent (102) were paid. I also expected the e-mail draft to offer the other sizes but it didn't, and it asked the customer what discount it wanted while I had anticipated that that would have been presented to the human with a suggestion based on the margin. Also, accounting accessed inventory once when it should have been unnecessary.

What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

It would have been simpler to have a single agent for the sequential tasks without parallel processing or going back and forth. For high priority sequential actions with a linear process, like paying rent, a single agent would likely be more efficient. However, for tasks with multiple threads, like checking inventory, checking balance and other debts, checking with the customer for alternatives, the multi-agent system is better.

Three new problems this team could solve with the tools we built

  1. A vendor requesting payment on an overdue bill.
  2. A customer asking to purchase an in-stock item.
  3. A request to list the sales value of current inventory.

Three new problems this team could not solve, and the tools and agents it would need

  1. Adding new items to inventory. This team could not manage requests about adding new items to inventory and would need a tool to categorize new items according to the existing template and add them including adding their values and margin and vendors.
  2. Questions about items that used to be in stock. This team does not have the capability to access inventory over time information so cannot answer questions from customers about inventory that used to be in stock and who the vendor was. We'd need baseline historical information and a decision making capability (likely with human involvement) as to whether we are or are no longer working with a vendor and/or whether that vendor still can make a product.
  3. Working with outside agents. This team cannot interact with outside agents (like a vendor's agent) to check information about availability of items and delivery time. It would need a tool for the MCP to interact with outside ones and rules for those interactions.
